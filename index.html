The house

Douma's silk money went into tall rooms and triple-arched windows, and in 1874 it went into this one. Four generations of the Chams family lived here; then, for eleven years, nobody did.

We spent three winters putting it back — the same limestone, the same red tiles from Marseille, new plumbing behind old walls, and a kitchen big enough to feed everyone at once. Eight rooms, a vaulted breakfast room for cold mornings, a terrace for the other nine months.

What a day here looks like

  1. 08:00

    Breakfast on the terrace. Labneh from Bchaaleh, eggs with kishk, last summer's fig jam, and coffee that keeps arriving until you say stop.

  2. 11:00

    The old souk is four minutes on foot — a hundred shops under stone arches, a saddle-maker, and a printer who still sets type by hand.

  3. 16:00

    Down through the olive terraces to the valley floor, or up the trail towards Tannourine. Both take about two hours and neither is difficult.

  4. 20:00

    We cook on Fridays and Saturdays — four courses, $28 a head, tell us the day before. Other evenings we will send you to Abou Elias for the best grilled chicken in the district.

Douma is the reason to come

Six hundred houses with red roofs, arranged around a horseshoe of terraced hillside at 1,150 metres. A souk that has worked without interruption since the 1800s. The six-thousand-year-old olive trees of Bchaaleh, fifteen minutes away. The Baatara gorge waterfall, thirty-five.
